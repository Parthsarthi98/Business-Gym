# Round 1: Partner, India-focused early-stage VC

1. Verdict: back with changes.

2. Biggest weakness: the moat is asserted, not shown. "Cross-merchant behaviour data" sounds like Power (Network Economies/Cornered Resource) but claims-fraud signals (repeat serial-returner IDs, damage-photo patterns) are largely genericisable and available to any vendor buying the same signal from payment gateways or logistics partners; they are not proprietary to ClaimZero by construction. Mid-market is also the segment most likely to churn to a bundled offering from Shopify/Zoho/Freshdesk once "claims AI" becomes a checkbox feature, and the largest potential ACV accounts are explicitly excluded as build-in-house. This is the classic India SaaS trap: real problem, thin contract value, no structural lock-in, stalls at $10-15M ARR (judgement).

3. Amendments:
a) Replace "small per-claim fee" with a revenue-share/savings-based fee (e.g. 15-20% of manpower cost actually displaced), tied contractually to auto-resolution rate, to push ACV toward six figures per large mid-market account.
b) Add a second layer: aggregate anonymised fraud/serial-returner signals into a shared consortium graph across customers (own the data network, not just per-customer learning). This is the only path to a real Network Economies power.

4. Score: 4/10.

5. Blocking objection: no, but funding is conditional on rewriting the moat and pricing before further diligence.

6. Factual question: What fraction of "auto-resolvable" claims can genuinely be closed by AI today without human review, measured on real customer data, not a demo? If under 60%, the pitch collapses into a workflow tool.

7. Best alternative: a vertical claims-and-chargeback specialist for one high-ACV niche (e.g. travel/ticket aggregators, where claim values and fraud stakes are much higher than fashion returns) could win on Scale Economies via specialised policy libraries faster than a horizontal mid-market play, though it forgoes the larger fashion/quick-commerce TAM (judgement).

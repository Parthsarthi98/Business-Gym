# Brief: ClaimZero (boardroom-lite, 27 September 2026)

## 1. Summary and verdict
ClaimZero is a real problem with a launchable product once its data design is rebuilt, but it is not yet a unicorn plan: India alone likely caps it at US$15-75M ARR, and the platforms it wants as partners would rather build it. **Verdict: back with changes; not investable until data access and a higher-value second market are proven.**

## 2. Final version
See `idea-v3.md`. In short: AI decides routine claims for Indian consumer businesses, sold through order, shipping and returns platforms and directly to brands. A person confirms every rejection for the first 90 days. Every rejection has a reason and a one-click human appeal. ClaimZero pays the merchant if wrong rejections exceed 1%. Cross-merchant fraud signals are consented, hashed scores that a reviewer sees, never raw phone, email or order history, and never an automatic reject. Pricing is a subscription plus a flat per-claim fee.

## 3. What changed from v1 and why
- **Raw identifier pooling across merchants removed.** You confirmed the design pools raw phone, email and order history. All four members called this unlawful under the DPDP Act without specific consent, and a reputational risk (a customer blacklisted at a store they never used). This was the biggest change.
- **Human-confirm ramp, appeal path, indemnity, published error rate.** The buyer would not sign without them.
- **Partner-led distribution plus direct contracts.** The platform head argued the data sits with order and shipping platforms, so going around them is a losing race.
- **Data access named as the first milestone,** not treated as solved.
- **The cross-merchant network is now a long-term advantage, not the day-one moat.** Opt-in data will be thin and biased: fraudsters are the least likely to opt in.

## 4. Final scores
| Member | R1 | R2 | R3 | Blocking? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D2C buyer (VP Customer Experience) | 6 | 4 | 6 | No, needs a 90-day pilot under 1% false rejection |
| Sceptical VC | 4 | 3 | 5 | **Yes** |
| Platform product head | 4 | 3 | 6 | No, but would build rather than partner |
| Legal and fraud expert | 6 | 4 | 7 | No, needs consent clause and re-identification test first |

Average 6.0. **Dissent:** the VC blocks further capital until there is a partner LOI on ClaimZero's terms and a higher-value vertical in pilot. The VC's estimate (judgement): 300-500 Indian buyers at US$50-150K a year gives US$15-75M ARR, short of the US$80-100M needed.

## 5. Open questions and evidence to gather
- Pilot numbers: what share of claims can close with no human, and how often are rejections overturned? (Unanswered.)
- Which platforms or payment gateways will give API data access, and on what terms? (Unanswered.)
- Which second market gets you past the India ceiling: travel and ticketing, payment disputes and chargebacks, or the same platforms abroad?
- Default sales channel: partners first (platform head) or direct first (buyer, VC)?
- Why would a platform partner rather than build? Asked directly, the platform head said they would build in one to two quarters.
- Can fraud prevention across merchants rely on a basis other than opt-in consent under the DPDP Act and its Rules? Ask counsel; the board did not settle this.

## 6. Pre-mortem risks and tests (next two weeks)
1. **No data access, so no product.** Get three target platforms or brands to a yes in principle on sharing order, delivery and payment data, before writing code.
2. **The revenue arithmetic does not reach unicorn scale.** Build a one-page model naming 20 real Indian buyers, their claim volumes and price. If even the optimistic case is under US$50M ARR, pick the second market now.
(The pre-mortem member saw the VC's estimate, so risk 2 is not fully independent.)

## 7. Board and blind spot
D2C buyer, sceptical VC, platform product head, legal and fraud expert (added at your request). No web research was run: round 1 had it switched on but no member searched, and you turned it off for later rounds. All competitor and legal claims are the members' judgement. Blind spots: no one represented the end consumer, and no one covered expansion outside India, which the unicorn goal now depends on.

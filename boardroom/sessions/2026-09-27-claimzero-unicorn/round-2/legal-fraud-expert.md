# Round 2: Partner, technology and consumer law; ex-head of fraud risk

1. Strongest point against my view: the Sceptical VC argues a shared risk score without a data network is just a workflow tool, and pushes a consortium graph across customers. I accept the network-economics logic but reject his fix. A consortium graph on raw pooled identifiers, which the author confirms is the current design (phone, email, order history pooled, no hashing, no per-principal opt-in), is not a defensible moat; it is a DPDP purpose-limitation breach waiting for a Data Protection Board complaint or a viral "why was I blacklisted at a store I never used" story. The network effect must be built on consented, derived risk scores: slower to compound, but the only version that survives one regulatory complaint.

2. Amendments: CX buyer's 90-day soft-decline ramp and indemnity clause: accept. VC's consortium graph on raw identifiers: reject as stated; modify to hashed/derived scores only, opt-in per data principal. Platform head's reposition as infra sold via OMS/logistics partners: accept; it narrows the liability chain to fewer, better-resourced contracting parties with proper data processing clauses.

3. Score: 4/10, down from 6. The moat is built exactly the way I flagged as highest-risk, with no pilot data and no consent language. This is a rebuild, not a wording fix.

4. Blocking objection: yes. Will not back a design that pools raw claimant identifiers across merchants without consent; not launchable in India as described.

5. Evidence that would change my mind: a drafted consent clause naming cross-merchant risk-score sharing as a distinct purpose, accepted by one pilot merchant's legal team, plus a technical design showing only hashed/derived scores leave the per-merchant data boundary.

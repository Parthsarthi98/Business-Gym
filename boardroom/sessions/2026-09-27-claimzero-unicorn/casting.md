# Casting

## Decision dimensions
1. Buyer demand and willingness to pay in Indian mid-market D2C.
2. Market size and contract value ceiling: can India alone support a unicorn, or is a global path needed?
3. **Differentiation against bundling (highest risk):** helpdesk AI agents (Freshworks, Zendesk, Intercom, Indian conversational AI firms) and returns or logistics platforms (Shiprocket, Unicommerce, returns apps) can add policy-based auto-refunds as a feature.
4. Decision accuracy, fraud and liability: who bears the cost of a wrong refund or a wrong rejection.
5. Data network effect: whether cross-merchant claimant data is legally usable and actually defensible under India's DPDP Act.
6. Integration and distribution: time to plug into order, payment, logistics and helpdesk systems.

## Board

**Seat 1: Buyer. VP Customer Experience, mid-size Indian D2C fashion and beauty brand (about Rs 500 Cr GMV, Bengaluru).** Runs a 60-person outsourced support team on Freshdesk and a returns app. Optimises for cost per ticket, CSAT, return-fraud losses and not upsetting loyal customers. Rejects anything that needs a long integration, adds another vendor without killing one, or makes wrongful rejections they must defend on social media. Frameworks: cost-per-contact and payback period, first-contact resolution and CSAT, build versus buy versus wait for the helpdesk vendor.

**Seat 2: Adversary. Partner at an India-focused early-stage VC fund, sceptical of India-only SaaS.** Has seen many Indian B2B SaaS firms stall at US$5-15M ARR. Optimises for a path to US$100M ARR and a venture-scale exit. Rejects low contract values, feature-not-company risk, and moats that are asserted rather than shown. Frameworks: bottom-up TAM from number of buyers times realistic ACV, "why now and why you", 7 Powers (which power, if any, is real). Must name the best alternative, including doing nothing.

**Seat 3: Expert on the highest-risk dimension. Head of Product at an Indian e-commerce enablement platform (shipping, returns and post-purchase stack serving 50,000+ sellers).** Could ship this as a feature to an existing base. Optimises for attach rate across the seller base, low marginal cost and sticky bundles. Rejects standalone tools that depend on data the platform already owns. Frameworks: feature versus product versus platform test, integration surface and data gravity, partner or compete mapping.

**Seat 4 (added at author's request): Legal, regulatory and fraud. Partner at a Mumbai technology and consumer-law practice, formerly head of fraud risk and compliance at a large Indian payments firm.** Advises e-commerce and fintech clients on the DPDP Act, the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, RBI refund timelines and grievance-officer duties. Has run refund-abuse and fraud-ring investigations. Optimises for lawful data use, defensible automated decisions and low fraud loss. Rejects cross-merchant claimant profiling without valid consent, opaque automated rejections with no appeal, and unclear liability. Frameworks: DPDP consent and purpose limitation, fraud loss versus false-positive cost, liability allocation in contracts.

## Runner-up
Head of Trust and Safety at a large Indian quick-commerce firm. Largely covered now by seat 4.

## Blind spot
No one represents the end consumer directly, and no one covers global expansion go-to-market (US or Southeast Asia buyers).

## Run settings
Board size 4. Research on (up to 2 web searches per member in round 1).

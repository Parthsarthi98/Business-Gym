# Idea v1: ClaimZero

**Mode:** improve
**Goal:** build this into a unicorn (valuation of US$1B or more), starting from India.
**Success test (inferred):** a credible path to roughly US$80-100M ARR within 7 to 8 years, with a defensible position that justifies a venture-scale multiple.
**Assumptions stated by main session:** pre-product or very early stage; no named customers yet; founding team and funding not specified.

## The proposal (neutral restatement)

ClaimZero is proposed as embedded, white-label AI infrastructure that resolves routine consumer claims for D2C enterprises. It reads the evidence, applies each enterprise's own policy, and issues decisions instantly, with people handling only edge cases.

**Problem.** Fashion e-commerce, quick-commerce and ticket aggregators handle high daily volumes of routine claims: payment debited but goods not delivered on time, wrong item, damaged item, insurance claims, leading to refund and return requests. Most enterprises open, check and resolve each claim by hand. This is repetitive and standardised, absorbs growing support headcount, takes hours or days and gives inconsistent outcomes. Hiring more agents is the only lever. Customers also lack a single place to claim and must chase the payment gateway, the product provider and the platform separately.

**Data points cited.** 25-40% of fashion orders returned online; 20-40% cash-on-delivery return-to-origin; 1.7 lakh complaints per month to the National Consumer Helpline, e-commerce the top category; India e-commerce US$125B, D2C nearing US$100B; quick-commerce growing 70-80% a year. Sources cited: Coresight, Unicommerce, NCH 2025, IBEF, Gartner.

**Why AI.** Ticketing tools organise and route claims but a person still resolves each one. A rules engine cannot read a photo or messy free text against policy. The AI reads evidence, applies policy, detects false claims and closes standard claims around the clock.

**Product flow.**
1. Customer files a claim inside the brand's own app or site (white-label).
2. Platform captures photos, receipts, order and delivery data and applies the enterprise's codified policy rulebook in a single queue.
3. Instant decision: refund, credit, replace or reject, consistent and auditable.
4. Only AI-flagged edge cases reach a human reviewer. Dashboard shows auto-resolution rate, cost and time savings, escalations.

**Target customer.** High-volume consumer businesses: D2C e-commerce, quick-commerce, flight and ticket aggregators, any enterprise with large volumes of standardised claims. The largest incumbents may build in-house; the target is mid-market enterprises with lean support teams that are unlikely to build it.

**Business model.** Tiered SaaS subscription scaled to claim volume plus a small per-claim resolution fee. Revenue tracks the manpower cost removed.

**Value claimed.** Fewer agents, claims resolved in seconds, faster rejection of fraud, better customer experience.

**Moat claimed.** Learns each customer's claim history to flag repeat or abusive claimants. More enterprises onboarded means richer privacy-preserving cross-merchant behaviour data, so claims are judged on evidence plus transaction-behaviour analytics. Accuracy improves and switching costs rise with the network. Routine claims grow with business, so revenue is recurring.

1. Strongest point against my view: the VC calls the ramp and consented scores cost items that slow the flywheel. Answer: they are the licence to operate. A faster flywheel built on a DPDP breach ends in a Data Protection Board order or a viral blacklisting story. Slower and lawful beats fast and dead.

2. v2 changes: consented hashed/derived scores, accept. Reviewer-only cross-merchant flag, accept. 90-day ramp with error-gated auto-reject, accept. Published false-rejection rate and indemnity, accept. Partner-channel GTM, accept (fewer counterparties to police). Pricing and vertical: not my lane.

3. Score: 7/10, up from 4. The rebuild resolves my round 2 blocker in design. Not 8+ because nothing is drafted or executed.

4. Blocking objection: no, conditional. Before any pilot: (i) draft the consent clause naming cross-merchant score sharing as a distinct, granular purpose, reviewed by counsel; (ii) confirm the technical boundary prevents re-identification from derived scores; (iii) grievance-officer logging and appeal timelines must meet the E-Commerce Rules, not just be "logged".

5. Fraud view on opt-in: opt-in will produce thin, adversely selected data. Fraud-prone consumers are least likely to opt in; honest repeat customers most likely. Expect a small, biased pool for months, useful to build reviewer trust, not a strong early moat. Evidence that would change my mind: a drafted consent clause accepted by one pilot merchant's legal team, plus early opt-in rate and fraud-flag precision from a live cohort.
